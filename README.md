# FS25_HorseDynamics

Makes the horses of a pasture follow you.

* **Right Ctrl + C**: calls the nearest horse (a horse of a pasture, or one that stands loose as a vehicle). Press again for each further horse.
* **Right Ctrl + V**: gives them all back.
* Console: `hdLead [start|stop|status]`.

A horse follows in bursts (a few seconds of walking, then it stands a while, then follows again); the gait grows with the distance (walk, canter, trot, gallop) and eases back as it closes in;
when something is in its way (a fence, a sprayer) it backs up and turns twice before it is given up on and released; it is given back by itself when it is at a stable and you are with it, so leading horses to a new stable is walking them there.

## Hand tools (in the shop, category "animals" hand tools: the one the game's horse brush is in)

* **Carrot** (15): a switch: the primary action calls every horse of the pasture you stand in (or the nearest horse pasture within 40 m) and also gathers the horse vehicles that stand loose within 80 m (released earlier, stuck somewhere); the same action again lets those horses go.
* **Leash** (25): the primary action takes the nearest horse (a pasture horse or one that stands loose) on the leash, but only one within 4 m (`HorseLead.LEASH_REACH_M`: you walk up to a horse, you do not summon it, that is the carrot's job; further away it says so); the same action again lets the leashed horses go.

The shop images are `assets/shop_icons.png` split in two. In-hand models: the carrot is the base game's own carrot mesh (`effects/unload/particle/carrot`) in a small wrapper i3d (`handTools/carrot/carrot.i3d`, hand placement copied from the horse brush, to be tuned in the game); the leash has no model of its own any more (the base game's rope props, a coil of `rope01`/`rope02`, were tried and were too big): in the hand it shows a rope draped over the hand while no horse is on it (`HorseRope.syncStub`): an inverted U, two straight legs of 25 cm (3 pieces each) and a half circle of 3.5 cm radius on top (8 pieces, so no joint turns the rope by more than 22.5 degrees), the apex at the tool, in the vertical plane facing the camera so it shows from the side (`STUB_RADIUS_M`, `STUB_LEG_M`; earlier versions: a closed ring, which from above looked like a hoop lying on the grass, then a parabola, whose apex bent under a centimetre across and showed as a hairpin with flat ends), and with a horse on it the rope itself runs from the hand to the horse. Icons were made with the pipeline of
`FS25_whatAmILookingAt/docs/icon_pipeline.md` (ImageMagick for the crop and resize, GIANTS textureTool for the BC7 DDS): `store/carrot.dds`, `store/leash.dds`, `icon.dds`.

**Pinned horses (between bushes, in a collider):** a led horse backs up and turns twice, is then put 3 m towards you once (the game's own `setWorldPosition`, which moves the character controller too), and only then given up on. A horse you ride that stays halted for 2.5 s is put 3 m behind itself automatically; `hdLead free` does the same for the nearest horse on demand.

**Mounting a following horse:** press the enter key on it as on any horse. The virtual rider is only a flag; the game sees the horse as free (it reports "controlled" only inside the one game function that
holds an unridden horse still), so mounting works; the horse is then dropped from the string and the others keep following you (on the horse).

**The leash is a real rope** (`scripts/HorseRope.lua`, confirmed in the game: it draws, the horse follows it): each leashed horse gets a rope to its bridle, drawn by the game's own winch rope (`ForestryRope`, the shape the forestry winch uses: a straight rope that the shader stretches between a link node and a target node every frame; the path has to be resolved with `Utils.getFilename`, the engine does not know `$data`). The target is the horse's rein node. A `ForestryRope` is always straight, so the sag is made of 6 pieces with points between them, put every frame on a hanging curve (the slack makes the sag, at most 35 cm, never below the ground; `HorseRope.SEGMENTS`, `SAG_MAX_M`). The rope starts at the leash in your hand; put the leash away and it stays, hanging from your chest, and when you mount a horse it hangs from the saddle: the horses are still led, so the rope is too. Let the horses go and the ropes are deleted. 1.5 cm thick (`HorseRope.DIAMETER`). Following: a horse that stands (arrived, or in a pause) turns in place to face you (`RIDE_TURN_RATE_STILL`), and when you ride, the followers keep about 2.4 m behind your horse instead of the carrot herd's 5 m plus a spread of 0 to 2.5 m of its own per horse (`RIDE_ARRIVE_M`, `RIDE_ARRIVE_SPREAD_M`: at 3.5 m the first horse stood with its mane in the camera) (`RIDE_ARRIVE_MOUNTED_M`) and do not pause far behind (`RIDE_KEEP_UP_MOUNTED_M`).

## How it works

The game swaps a pasture animal for a Rideable vehicle when a player rides it (`PlaceableHusbandryAnimals:startRiding`). Called with no player, that gives a horse vehicle nobody rides. A per-instance
`getIsControlled` that says "yes" stops the server holding it still (`Rideable:onUpdateInterpolation`), and the script sets gait and steer itself each frame. A rideable near a pasture goes back into it
(`doHusbandryCheck`). The horse's heading (`currentTurnAngle`, the game's own field) is turned towards you at about 400 degrees a second while it moves (`RIDE_TURN_RATE`), because the game's own turning (45 degrees a second) draws a gentle arc straight into the wall next to a gate. Proven in the game with the AnimalLab prototype (`~/my_mods/FS25_AnimalLab`, which stays the lab for probing the engine's animal functions).

**How a led horse is held (`hdLead style halter|tack|reins|rope`).** The default, `halter`: a rope halter on the bare horse, made of the same winch-rope pieces as the lead: a noseband loop round the muzzle, a crown strap that runs from the top of the head behind the ears down each side to a small steel ring on the cheekbone and on past the jowl to under the throat, a cheek strap from a second small ring on the noseband's side to that same ring, and a knot under the chin that the lead is tied to (noseband of 12 pieces, 70 pieces in all, points under the world root placed from the head bone `Head_M` every frame, pieces tied like the lead's and updated every frame by the rope code, built when a horse is led and deleted when it is let go; `hdLead halterinfo` reports how many pieces were made, loaded and tied and where the head bone, the points and a piece are, to see why a halter does not show). The loops were measured from the horse mesh (an OBJ export of the whole horse), in the head bone's space (+y forward along the face, +x up, z sideways): the muzzle at y = 0.35 is 0.27 m tall and 0.20 m wide, behind the jaw at y = 0.17 it is 0.33 by 0.27 m; `hdLead halter nose y centre vertical lateral` moves the noseband while you look. `tack`: the horse shows its saddle, bridle and reins (the reins at rest; the bridle is part of the saddle mesh, a halter alone cannot be shown) and the rope runs to the bridle's bit ring, a node on the head bone at (-0.0983, 0.4425, 0.07) worked out from the bridle's geometry (`hdLead ring x y z`). `reins` links the game's own reins to the leash in your hand: tried in the game, the skinned reins mesh stretches into long angular shapes beyond about a metre. `rope` is the rope to the horse's rein node on the bare horse (that node sits on the body, not at the head). When the horse is let go the halter is deleted, and the reins are put back and the tack hidden (`Rideable:unlinkReins`), but not for a horse a player has mounted.

**Looking at the gear: freeze (Right Ctrl + F, or `hdFreezeHorse`).** The first version of this only stopped the turning in place, and the horse still walked: it followed the lead and backed away when you walked into it. The honest one is a freeze: a toggle that makes every led horse stand completely still (no following, no backing away, no turning) until you press it again, or until no horse is led any more. Putting the harness on a horse tells you about the key. `hdLead face on|off` (only the turning in place) is still there. A horse that stands (arrived, or waiting) turns to face you, so you never see its side. `hdLead face off` makes a standing horse stay as it stands (the default is on: `HorseLead.RIDE_TURN_RATE_STILL`), so you can walk round it and look at the halter.

**Halter fit (measured, then gravity).** The loops come from the horse mesh in the head bone's space: the noseband at 0.35 along the face (0.20 m wide, 0.27 m tall), the crown not a loop at all any more but a path (a real halter's crownpiece is not an ellipse, and the head is not either: the jowl is 0.15 to 0.17 m half wide where the skull behind the ears is 0.06 to 0.11, so the old slanted ellipse ran 5 cm inside the jaw and the cheek piece grazed the cheek by 3 mm). From a reference photo of a real halter: the cheek strap, the strap up to the ear and the strap down under the jaw meet at ONE ring at the cheekbone, at an angle, so there is a 16 piece steel ring (8 pieces, 0.02 m radius) at (-0.045, 0.185, 0.145) each side, and the three straps end on the ring points nearest to where they come from. The strap down the cheek runs 30 degrees forward of the head's straight down, in the direction the noseband's lower leg takes (from two screenshots with red lines drawn on them: nearly vertical on screen, which is forward of the head's own down because the head is pitched; `hdLead halter jaw degrees` tunes it, and a strap runs along the bottom each side from the noseband's lower corner back to the lower end of that strap, as a real halter has between the two (a red line in a screenshot asked for it), 0 is straight down; the first versions went straight back and then veered back), and the noseband passes through a ring of the same size as the cheek ring (0.016 m: two sizes did not help; this one 2 mm further to the front, in line with the strap sideways) at each side where the cheek strap is tied on. The noseband is not straight in side view either (a photo of a real halter: the strap goes from the top of the nose back to the side ring and on down to the jaw, so top, ring and jaw make an angle): its side points are shaped as a drawing over a screenshot asked: the front ring sits LOW on the side of the nose (120 degrees round the loop from the top, near the corner of the mouth), the loop is pulled back 14 cm from the top to the ring and stays there down to the chin, so the lower leg falls straight down under the jaw and the upper leg slopes up over the nose bridge; the muzzle is taller (0.2 per metre) and wider (0.31 per metre) further back, so the pulled-back part stays outside the head (5 to 21 mm). The cheek strap is a straight line from that ring to the cheek ring (15 to 16 cm), the strap along the bottom runs from the chin knot, on both sides. `hdLead halter bend`, `HorseRope.halter.nose.ringAngle`. The noseband itself moved forward from y 0.35 to y 0.43, on the nose just behind the nostrils (the first versions sat too high up the face, a red line in a screenshot): the muzzle there is 0.085 half wide and 0.119 half high, centre -0.043 (measured at y 0.40 and 0.45: 0.090 / 0.124 and 0.081 / 0.114) and stand wider there (the head widens: 0.31 m per m), `hdLead halter bend d` sets it, 0 is a straight noseband. Every path point stands 5 to 15 mm outside the measured width of the head at its height. The noseband clearance is 2 mm; its polygon has 12 pieces. `hdLead halter clearance d` moves the crown, the cheek straps and the rings further off (or closer to) the head if one still clips. The top of a loop rests on the skin, below the head the rope hangs slack: each point has a weight (0 at the top of its loop, 1 at the bottom) and sags `slack` * weight metres along WORLD-down (`worldDirectionToLocal` of the head bone every frame, so a lowered head still has the slack hang down): 1.2 cm below the noseband (the chin knot with it), 2.5 cm below the crown. `hdLead halter nose|crown y centre vertical lateral [tilt]` and `hdLead halter slack nose crown` move them live.

**Halter offset (`hdLead halteroffset x y z`).** The halter's first look in the game was with the whole halter offset 0.35 m sideways, so it could not hide inside the head: it showed (two loops, two cheek pieces, the lead tied to the knot under the chin), so it is on the head by default now (`HorseRope.halterOffset` 0 0 0), with 2 cm of clearance round the measured head (`HorseRope.halter.margin`).

**The rope's material (`hdLead material hemp|steel`, `hdLead tint r g b`).** The game's rope shapes are steel grey with a glossy cable map, and the shader has no colour parameter (only an emissive one, which follows the normal map's strands: a tint through it looks like candy stripes, so the default is none). So each rope piece, once its shape has loaded, gets a copy of the game's rope material (same shader and normal map) with two tiny solid-colour textures of our own: a hemp-tan diffuse and a matte gloss map (`textures/rope_hemp_diffuse.dds`, `rope_hemp_specular.dds`, made from the `.png` next to them with the GIANTS textureTool). The gloss map's channels were read from the game's own maps: red is smoothness and blue metalness (the steel cable has 189 / 208 / 255, the hemp rope prop 43 / 212 / 0; ours is 43 / 212 / 0). It is done with the engine's own calls (`setMaterialDiffuseMapFromFile`, `setMaterialGlossMapFromFile`, `setMaterial`, the way `VehicleMaterial.lua` does it) and caught: if a call fails the rope stays steel and the log says so once. `steel` puts the game's own material back.

**On the leash a rope is as long as it is.** A leashed horse never stops to wander and pause (that is for the carrot: those horses have a choice): it stays within the rope, `HorseRope.LENGTH_M` (3 m). Distances are from the horse's root node, the middle of its body: its nose is about 1.5 m ahead of that (`HEAD_REACH_M`), so it stops 3.4 m from you (its nose about 1.9 m away, the rope hanging slack), walks on only when you are 3.8 m away, and trots or canters to catch up when the rope would pull taut (`RIDE_GAITS_LEASH`, ordered by the horse's own top speeds). The first version stopped at 2 m and put the horse's face in the player's. The rope sags only with slack and is straight at the end of its length. A player who walks up to a horse (in front of it, closer than 2.7 m, its nose 1.2 m from him) makes it step back, until he is 3.1 m away (`RIDE_BACKOFF_M`); not when he is mounted, and not when he is at its side.

**Tack (probe).** The rope ends at the horse's rein node, which sits on the body, not at a halter: the game shows the saddle and the bridle as ONE node (`equipmentNode`, `setEquipmentVisibility`) and the reins as another (`reinsNode`, linked to the rider's hands). Whether the model has the bridle as a node of its own is only visible in the game: `hdTack list` writes the tree of the tack nodes of the nearest horse vehicle to the log, `hdTack <node name> on|off` shows or hides any node of it (also `hdTack show` / `hide` for all of it).

**Stables.** A horse the carrot called is handed over to the game at a stable when you are with it (the game's own check then takes it in). A horse on the leash never is: you hold it until you let go, so walking it through a pasture's gate does not take it away from you.

**Gates.** The leash click is for a fence gate when you look at it (`scripts/HorseGate.lua`): the gate is within 4.5 m of you (its door or its trigger) and the view ray passes within 1.8 m of its door, hinge or trigger. With a horse on the rope it opens or closes the gate instead of letting the horse go. With no horse on the rope it does the same when the gate is met before the horse the click would take along the ray: a horse you look at in front of the gate wins, and a gate you are merely standing next to never counts (so with a harness or a leash aimed at a horse beside a gate, the horse is the one). With no camera ray (LOOK off) the nearest gate in reach counts, as it used to. The game's own `setDirection` is used on every leaf of the gate (the same call as its key), the farm check of the game is respected. Both fence systems are covered (pasture fences and the older fence placeables). To let a horse go next to a gate, look away from the gate and click again.

## The harness (a hand tool, first contraption)

The **Harness** tool (shop, hand tools, animals, 120) puts a draught harness on the horse beside you and takes it off again, and does nothing else: the halter and the harness are independent. A horse on the leash (it wears the halter and follows you) gets the harness on top, so it has both. Any other horse within 4 m is taken as a horse that STAYS (`ride.stay`: it becomes a vehicle so that it can wear things, it stands where it is, no halter, no rope, it follows nobody and is not given back at a stable) and wears only the harness; take the harness off and it goes back to its pasture. The leash tool takes a staying horse along (it gets the halter and follows, and keeps the harness); letting go of the leash does not give a harnessed horse back, it stays where it is wearing the harness. Right Ctrl + F (freeze) is for looking at a led horse. In the hand the harness tool shows a collar: a closed ring of 12 thick rope pieces (0.05 m, 13 cm radius) hanging from the hand in the plane that faces the camera (`HorseRope.syncCollar`, as the leash shows its loop of rope), because the tool itself has no model. It is made of the same rope pieces as the halter, hung on four body bones of the horse so it follows the walk: a **collar** (an ellipse round the neck's base, leaning back at the top, a steel ring each side), a **girth** round the barrel just behind the elbows with a thick **saddle pad** on the back and a steel **tug ring** each side (where a cart's shafts will go), a **neck strap** from the collar's top back along the withers to the pad (a red line in a screenshot: it was missing), a **loin strap** over the back to the croup, **hip straps** down both sides to the **breeching rings** and a **breeching** strap behind the buttocks, a **false martingale** (the belly rope: from the bottom of the collar down between the front legs and along the underside to the girth, the strap that carries the pull; another program's picture of this harness had it), a lighter **back band** round the barrel behind the girth, and a **trace** each side from the collar's ring along the shoulder and the flank through the tug ring and on behind the horse, where its end hangs free (the cart takes those later). 131 rope pieces, so it is heavier than the halter: a spike, to get a reasonable contraption on a horse.

The measurements come from the horse mesh without its saddle (an OBJ export of the whole horse minus the saddle's vertices), in the model's space (+z forward, +y up): the barrel at z 0.2 is 0.87 to 1.62 high and 0.30 half wide, the rump ends at z -0.8, the topline is 1.68 at the croup, the neck crest at the collar 1.9. Each point belongs to a bone (`Chest_M` for the collar and the shoulder part of the traces, `Spine1_M` for girth, pad and tugs, `RootPart1_M` and `Root_M` for the back and the rump) and is turned into that bone's space with its rest pose (`HorseHarness.bones`, worked out from `horse.i3d`); like the halter's, the points hang under the world root, are put at their bone every frame and hang down by their `slack` along world-down (the breeching sags 3 cm, the free ends of the traces 30 cm).

`hdHarness info` lists every measurement, `hdHarness set <path> <number>` changes one while you look (`set collar.a 0.4`, `set girth.z 0.25`, `set girth.profile.3.1 0.30`, `set hip.ring.1 0.33`; the harness is rebuilt), `hdHarness reset` puts the defaults back. The harness goes when the horse is let go (the leash tool); taking it off with the tool leaves the horse on its leash.

Not yet: reins, bridle, a cart on the traces, a harness that stays on a pasture horse, leather textures (it is hemp for now).

## Leather straps (`hdLead gear leather|rope`)

The halter and the harness are flat brown leather straps by default (a suggestion: "replace the rope with leather straps, brown elongated rectangles, and you might build a convincing bridle"). How: a winch-rope piece is a tube between two points, and the shader scales its x and y alike (`ropeShader.xml`: `position.x *= size; position.y *= size`), so a piece that has loaded gets `setScale(shape, 1, thickness / width, 1)`: a flat ellipse, 4 mm thick (at most a fifth of the width), 2 cm wide on the halter, 3 to 5 cm on the harness (the shader's size parameter is the diameter, not the radius: the first version halved every width and the straps came out as thin cords) (`halter.strapWidth`, the `strap` of each harness part). The game's own update always turns a piece with world-up as its "up", so a flat strap would lie flat in the world, edge on where it runs down a side: a strap piece has its own update (`HorseRope.strapUpdate`) that uses the skin's normal instead, out from the head's axis on the halter, out from the body's axis (a line along z at height 1.30) on the harness, out from the rump's centre for the breeching, turned from the bone's space to world every frame. The material is leather: `textures/rope_leather_diffuse.dds` (98 / 62 / 36), the gloss map, and a flat normal map (`setMaterialNormalMapFromFile`: the rope's own normal map is the strands, which would show as a twist on a strap). The rings stay steel, the padded collar and the saddle pad are round leather (thick), the lead and the rope in the hand stay hemp. `hdLead gear rope` puts back the round rope on the halter and the harness.

To try in the game: whether the scaled tube is thin (the node scale is applied after the shader), whether the straps lie flat on the skin, and the corners: a flat strap shows the wedge gap at a polygon's corner where a round rope hides it (more pieces or an overlap would help).

A horse in reach wins over a joint or a log in view. The separate hand winch left the shop (`showInStore` false); the two that your savegame already has still work, so there is one tool less to scroll through.

## The horse pulls (the hitch, `HorseHitch.lua`; needs FS25_WinchTow)

The harness finally has something to do. Put it on a horse, lead the horse (leash tool, the harness stays), stand with a trailer's drawbar eye in view (the winch's green ring shows on it) and click with the harness tool: two trace ropes run from the ends of the harness's traces to the eye, and where the horse walks, the trailer comes. Click again and it is let go.

How it works, no magic: the harness has a hook, a little steel ring where the two traces meet, and the trailer's drawbar eye is pinned to the middle of it, the way a tractor's hitch holds a trailer's eye (the first versions were a rope that pulled only when taut, then a swingletree and a chain, and the wagon wandered about in S curves behind the horse). The pin is in the horse's own frame and at the height of the traces' ends, so the eye is lifted off the ground to the harness (the support leg is retracted). It is a stiff spring and damper on the trailer's joint body: the speed it wants is the horse's own plus 4 per second per metre of error, the force is the whole vehicle's mass times 12 per second times the speed it is short of, at most 10 m/s2 and never more than 4 kN (a horse is no tractor: a 14 t tractor is accelerated at 0.3 m/s2 if the ground lets it move at all, a load that stays more than 4 m behind the pin for 4 s makes it say it cannot move this load; the gains are lowered for a heavy load so the force limit is not hit on every bit of noise, which made it bounce). A horse that pulls never gallops (a gallop becomes a trot); and the PLAYER is slowed by the load: your walking and running speed is capped (a cap on the game's walk state, shared with the hand winch of FS25_WinchTow: the tool multipliers of the game cannot slow a runner, running is lerp(4, 7) with a floor of the whole walk speed) at the speed the winch gives a load of that weight, so the whole string slows down instead of the horse stopping and starting. It holds in both directions, a horse that steps back pushes the wagon back, and the wagon swings round the pin on its own wheels, with that swing of its heavy body damped (a pendulum with nothing against it swung for ever). A trailer nobody sits in has its brake down, so the brake is let off while the horse walks and put back when it stands. A harnessed horse can be ridden (until a wagon has a seat), and from the saddle it works like a tractor: back up to a trailer's drawbar eye (or a machine's hitch) and the game's own attach key (Q) shows "Hitch", "Unhitch" while something is hitched; mounting keeps the harness and what it pulls, only the halter and the lead go; you drive with the game's own controls (a gallop is a canter while it pulls), and when you get off it stands in its harness again. `HorseDynamics.BLOCK_MOUNT_HARNESSED = true` refuses the mount key on a harnessed horse instead. For the first 40 s after hitching the log has one line a second saying what the pin does (`hitch: pin off by ...`).

**The real joint (`HorseJoint.lua`), and the glue as the spare tyre.** The glue above works, but a script that reacts is not a hitch: the wagon never felt like it hung on anything. So the horse now gets what a tractor has, an attacher joint of its own. The vehicle type "horse" gets the game's AttacherJoints specialization when the types are validated (the way FS25_FollowMe adds one), a joint node is made at the hook at runtime, the game's own loader reads `xml/horseJoint.xml` (`lowerRotLimit="25 75 75"`, like a tractor's), and the trailer is attached with the game's own `attachImplementFromInfo`: the physics joint, the support leg, the brake, all the game's. Press Q at a drawbar eye (or click with the harness tool) and a trailer with an input attacher joint is hitched in a tidy order, the same one a tractor does: the support leg goes up, the tongue is lifted on its hinge to the height of a tractor's joint (0.81 m, measured; every trailer is made to hang on that), the wagon is walked to the hook and stops where the eye meets it, and only then does the game's joint take over. The hook ring of the harness rides on the eye exactly, so the traces always end on it. Anything the game cannot attach this way (a machine's own hitch, a refusal) falls back to the rope glue. The same key unhitches.

Some things that took a while and are worth knowing: the joint's frame has to look the way the eye's node looks in the trailer (the Wooden Trailer's eye is turned 90 degrees in its model, a fixed 180 made it jerk when you turned left and not when you turned right); a wagon nobody sits in has its brake down, so the brake is let off every frame while it hangs on the joint; the horse's body is moved by the game in teleport steps and has no speed as far as the physics is concerned, so the horse's body is given the speed the game moves it at (`HorseHitch.FEED_VELOCITY`; without it a wagon trailed behind by an amount that grew with the gait).

Console (`hdJoint`): `info | add | attach | detach | remove | rot <degrees> | at <x> <y> <z> | show on|off | tractor | dump | height <m> | trace`. `dump` prints the height of the attacher joint you look at (that is where the 0.81 came from), `trace` writes one line per frame for a few seconds (frame time, the horse's speed, the wagon's speed, the gap to the eye), `show on` draws the joint's rings. The log after a hitch has a once-a-second line for a minute (`joint: watch: ...`).

**Careful with saving.** A savegame written while a wagon hangs on the horse's joint names a joint that only exists while it is hitched. Until that is handled (a save file of our own for the harness and the hitch is the plan), unhitch before you save.

Not yet: steering by a driver (the horse follows the leash, the trailer follows the horse), a heavy load (an ox would be nice), a horse that backs up on purpose, logs on the winch rope, the same joint for the player's hand (a hand-pulled wagon is still the winch's script), and keeping the harness and the hitch across a save.

### Letting go of the leash

Inside a pasture the halter comes off and the horse goes back in, as ever. Outside, it stays: the lead rope goes (your hands let go) and the horse stands there in its halter (and its harness, and the trailer behind it). The leash tool takes it again; walk it into a pasture and let go there to take the halter off. (A savegame reload forgets the halter: the horse stands loose again.)

## The annoying bits are settings
Fun once, annoying later, so the options that make horses a pain have switches on the real settings page (ESC, Settings, General, section "Horse Dynamics"; saved in `modSettings/FS25_HorseDynamics/config.xml`, `HorseSettings.lua`, the pattern of Immersive Weathering's page): **a pulling horse never gallops** (on), **loads slow you down** (on: your walk and run are held to the horse's own top speed while it pulls: no slower than the horse, whatever the load), **a horse is no tractor** (off: the horse's pull is limited to about 4 kN, so only a small plough or cultivator gets going). The punishment for cheaping out on the tractor early is the game's own: no AI helper and no steering assist behind a horse.

**A horse pulls a log.** Hook a log with the leash (the winch easter egg above), then look at a harnessed horse within 4 m and click: the log is handed to the horse, its traces end at the log's strap, and it pulls it as it pulls a wagon (same speed limits, the player is slowed to match). The leash's click, or the harness tool's, lets the log go; a log that is cut or belted meanwhile is let go by itself. A log is no wagon: no tow bar, no keys, and it is not "tipped over" when it lies on its side.

**Two ways to tie a log to a horse.** (1) The leash: hook the log first (it is hooked at the point you look at, with the game's strap and hook), then look at the harnessed horse: a green ring appears round its hook ring, a click ties the log there. (2) The harness tool: click a harnessed horse once to take its rope in hand, then look at a log: the ring shows on the trunk, a click ties it to that horse at the point you looked at. Either way the log is dragged on a rope (`HorseHitch.dragLog`): a pull on the point you tied it at when it is further than 2.5 m from the hook ring, never a push, never a speed override of the log's middle (the first version drove the log like a wagon and swung it round the horse).

**The wire keeps its length.** The cable from the harness ring to the log is a winch wire of a fixed length (5 m when tied): it hangs in a curve between its ends and what is not needed to span the gap lies on the ground, it never stretches. **Ctrl+M** lets wire out and **Ctrl+K** takes it in (held, 1.5 m a second, between 2 and 12 m; rebind them in the settings: "Let more wire out" and "Take wire in"). The log is pulled only when it is further away than the wire is long; further than the wire plus 2.5 m (stuck) the horse stops until it is within the wire plus 1 m again.

**The leash route works for vehicles too.** Hook a trailer's drawbar eye (or any joint) with the leash as before, then look at a harnessed horse within 4 m: the same green ring appears round its hook ring, and the click hands the trailer to it: it is hitched exactly as if you had clicked the joint with the harness tool (the support leg, the lift, the game's own joint when it can take it). The leash's click unhitches it again (the harness tool's click too). So a log, a wagon and a tractor all go the same two ways: tool on the thing first then the horse, or the horse first then the thing.

## Saving what the horses wear and pull
`scripts/HorseSave.lua` writes `<savegame>/horseDynamics.xml` when the game saves: for every led or harnessed horse vehicle its unique id, the harness and its gear, the halter (a leashed horse comes back standing with the halter on), and the hitch (the pulled vehicle's unique id, joint kind and joint index; a hitched log is not saved). When the mission starts the file is read, and each horse is restored as soon as its vehicle exists, its harness rig is built and the joint is found (90 s at most; records whose vehicle never shows up are dropped). The joint lookup uses `g_currentMission.winchTow.jointsOf` (FS25_WinchTow publishes it). Log lines: `[HorseDynamics] save:`.
